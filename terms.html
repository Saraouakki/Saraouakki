Have this reviewed — and fix it on the storefront

Two gaps to close. First, the storefront currently displays prices in US dollars, while you deliver to the GCC and Morocco and collect cash in local currency — customers must be told, before they order, the exact amount in the currency the courier will actually collect. Ambiguity about the final price is a classic consumer-protection complaint. Second, tax treatment differs across these markets: several GCC states apply VAT and Morocco applies its own regime, and rules on displaying tax-inclusive prices vary. Get advice per country on VAT registration thresholds, tax display requirements, and who is liable for import duty on goods shipped from abroad.

Have this reviewed

Fill in delivery windows you can genuinely meet. If stock ships from an overseas supplier rather than local warehousing, real transit times can run considerably longer than customers expect, and several jurisdictions give a consumer the right to cancel if delivery exceeds a stated or reasonable period. Ask what maximum delivery period applies in each market and what you must disclose upfront.

Have this reviewed before adding any penalty

Many COD sellers want to charge a fee for repeatedly refused deliveries, since the shipping cost is real and unrecoverable. Whether you can do that — and how it must be disclosed before checkout to be enforceable — depends on local consumer law, and an undisclosed or unfair penalty clause is likely to be void. Do not add a refusal fee to this section without advice.

Have this reviewed

This is the section most likely to be legally inadequate, because minimum return and cancellation rights are set by law and cannot be shortened by your terms — a policy that offers less than the statutory minimum is simply unenforceable, and publishing one invites disputes. Confirm the mandatory return window and the mandatory refund deadline for each country you serve, and check that your hygiene exclusion is drawn as narrowly as the law requires. Also settle the practical question of how cash refunds are actually returned to a customer, since COD gives you no payment method to reverse.

Have this reviewed before selling the extended warranty

The extended-warranty add-on needs attention on two fronts before it goes live. Substantively, this page has to state what it actually covers, for how long, what it excludes, who honours a claim, and how a customer makes one — an extended warranty sold without those terms is close to unsellable and a likely source of complaints. Structurally, a paid promise to repair or replace a product over time is treated in some jurisdictions as a regulated insurance or service-contract product, which can require licensing or an underwriter. Ask specifically about this add-on in each market before charging for it. Gift packaging raises none of these issues.
